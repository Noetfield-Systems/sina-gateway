# Sina Engineering — service launch playbook

Positioning: Applied AI. Global Software. Independent engineering practice, not a translation agency or a claim of ownership of client ventures. Offers are bounded professional engagements, not already-built SaaS products. Catalog: /services/catalog.json. Prices in CAD are introductory hypotheses, not historical market evidence; delivery estimates start after scope/access confirmation. No client logos, case studies, certifications or language fluency claims without evidence.

## Market entry
Lead with LocaleReady Audit for SaaS CTOs and globalization leads, LanguageBench for ML/product leads, and LocOps Connect for localization providers. Secondary offers: Global AI Pilot, Applied AI Build, Agent Workflow Pilot, InferenceTune. Signals: new locale launches, i18n hiring, TMS migration, multi-language AI rollout, slow inference, manual localization handoffs. Use public corporate contact routes or permissioned introductions. Respect local electronic marketing rules, opt-outs and platform policies. No scraped personal lists or automated mass messaging.

## Distribution
- Professional profile: AI/ML Software Engineering | Localization & Internationalization | Multilingual AI.
- Landing: /services/; focused links: /services/?offer=locale-ready, /services/?offer=language-benchmark, /services/?offer=locops-connect.
- Technical content: reproducible pseudo-localization demo, two-locale eval example, TMS-to-CI demo; label synthetic examples, do not imply client results.
- Partner route: offer engineering subcontracting to localization providers; agree scope, confidentiality and client boundaries.
- Outreach example: "I work on localization engineering and multilingual AI. If your team is preparing a new locale or evaluating AI quality across languages, I offer a scoped technical audit with reproducible findings and a remediation plan. Would a brief technical discussion be useful?" Tailor to an actual public signal; no fictitious observation.
- Offer page CTA: choose offer and submit project inquiry. Existing API persists a development inquiry with offer ID in the message. No separate CRM.

## Conversion and delivery
1. Record inquiry ID, offer ID, company, problem, next action, date and owner in existing authorized CRM or inquiry workflow. Do not store private customer records in this public repo.
2. Qualify: concrete technical problem, buyer authority, desired outcome, access readiness, budget/timeline; reply with relevant scoped offer and clarify exclusions.
3. Send proposal: scope, deliverables, acceptance tests, timeline, currency, taxes, dependencies, commercial terms and confidentiality. Suggested default for fixed engagements: 50% deposit and 50% at acceptance, subject to mutually agreed terms.
4. Invoice through existing authorized billing flow; record payment receipt before marking paid. No online payment link or automatic invoice is configured by this PR.
5. Execute on agreed access; document reproducible artifacts and test results. Agree change order for additional work.
6. Handoff, acceptance, balance collection, optional remediation or continuing engineering engagement. Request permission before publishing evidence.

## Operating metrics (initial 30-day goals, not forecasts or actual results)
20 qualified target organizations, 10 relevant personalized conversations, 3 scoped proposals, 1 paid engagement. Track qualified conversations, proposals sent, accepted agreements, invoices issued, cash collected, gross margin, delivery acceptance and repeat work. Weekly review: revise offer copy, scope and price based on actual objection and conversion evidence. Avoid measuring impressions as revenue.

## Launch verification
Run CI; merge and verify deployment; anonymously open landing and offer deep links on desktop/mobile; confirm actual API receipt and authorized DB record for a synthetic inquiry; verify invoice route before claiming checkout. Track production vs implementation status explicitly.
